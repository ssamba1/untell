# What an AI-detector verdict is worth: measurements, November 2026

*untell technical report, 2026-11-23. Every figure below links to the report card, data file or
script that produced it; all are reproducible from this repository.*

## Summary

1. **Seven commercial detectors (2023 versions) flagged 52–76% of TOEFL essays by non-native
   writers and 0–12% of US 8th-graders' essays**, recomputed from the scores Liang et al. recorded;
   untell reproduces the paper's 61% average (62.0%).
2. **The most-downloaded open detector, Desklib, flags 52.7% of TOEFL essays and 51% of Stanford
   students' pre-2022 abstracts at its default threshold**, while separating AI from human text
   with AUROC 0.984. Fakespot shows the same pattern (65.9%, 100%).
3. **One trailing space reverses every "AI" verdict `hc3_roberta` gives on short text** — 36 of 36
   flagged TOEFL essays and 88 of 88 flagged GPT-3 abstracts.
4. **Hard-wrapping text at 72 columns moved three of untell's own detectors by up to 1.0** on
   identical words.
5. **untell's own verdict flagged 92% of TOEFL essays and 49–73% of every race group of US
   students.** Recalibrated on held-out pre-2022 text it flags 15% and 2–7%, at the cost of halving
   recall on GPT-3 text.
6. **No single threshold is fair across writer populations.** Thresholds fitted to 5% false
   positives on one human corpus left Desklib flagging 36% of TOEFL essays and Fakespot 64%.
7. **On essays from a 2024 open model, recall reorders the detectors.** Desklib and Fakespot caught
   60 of 60 — the same two that flag 53–66% of TOEFL essays. untell's calibrated verdict caught 15%
   under v2; v3, which adds ModernBERT, catches 50%. OpenAI's RoBERTa detector caught none.
   ModernBERT caught 78% while flagging no TOEFL essay — the best trade-off measured here. On a
   second 2024 model (SmolLM2) the order flipped: OpenAI's detector caught 90%, ModernBERT 48%,
   and untell (v2 and v3), Desklib and Fakespot all 60.

## 1. Commercial detectors, 2023 versions

From the per-essay scores Liang et al. recorded, flagged at ≥ 0.5
([cards](observatory/liang-2023/cards/), [summary](observatory/liang-2023/summary.md)):

| detector | TOEFL (n=91) | US 8th grade (n=88) |
|---|---:|---:|
| GPTZero | 52% | 0% |
| Originality.ai | 76% | 1% |
| ZeroGPT | 53% | 1% |
| Sapling | 68% | 5% |
| Crossplag | 52% | 12% |
| Quillbot | 75% | 9% |
| OpenAI RoBERTa (hosted) | 58% | 9% |

These are early-2023 versions. Current versions were not measured: no API keys were available.

## 2. Open detectors from Hugging Face

Five of the most-installed, each run as its model card says, on 1,194 pre-2022 human texts and
352 GPT-3 texts ([leaderboard](observatory/open-detectors.md)):

| detector | AUROC | AI recall at 0.5 | TOEFL flagged | CS224N abstracts flagged |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| desklib/ai-text-detector-v1.01 | 0.984 | 98.0% | 52.7% | 51.0% |
| fakespot-ai/roberta-base-ai-text-detection-v1 | 0.984 | 99.7% | 65.9% | 100% |
| rasbt/ai-text-detector-modernbert | 0.929 | 21.0% | 0% | 0.7% |
| openai-community/roberta-large-openai-detector | 0.770 | 16.2% | 15.4% | 3.4% |
| PirateXX/AI-Content-Detector | 0.759 | 8.8% | 19.8% | 0% |

The two strongest separators carry the worst default thresholds. Formatting-only edits flipped
Desklib's verdict on 8.5% of variants; one trailing space cleared 22 of Fakespot's 62 TOEFL flags.
Among US students, Fakespot flagged White writers (7.7%) more often than Black writers (1.8%), and
Desklib the reverse (3.9% vs 6.2%).

## 3. Verdicts decided by typography

- **Trailing space** ([write-up](observatory/trailing-space.md)): on texts short enough to score in
  one window, appending one space reversed every `hc3_roberta` "AI" verdict (36/36 human TOEFL
  essays, 88/88 GPT-3 abstracts); AUROC fell from 0.784 to 0.326.
- **Hard wrapping and quote style**: re-scoring six essays with the same words hard-wrapped at 72
  columns moved MAGE by up to 1.000, `hc3_roberta` by 0.92 and `roberta_openai` by 0.85;
  curly → straight quotes moved `hc3_roberta` by 0.52. untell now normalises both before scoring,
  and its verdict flips on 0 of 55 formatting-only variants (previously 6 of 55).

## 4. Calibrating a verdict on held-out human text

untell's own five-model verdict (`max ≥ 0.30`) flagged 57% of 394 human essays. Each detector was
put on its own percentile scale among 868 pre-2022 human documents, with the cut fixed at 5% on
that fit corpus before any evaluation ([method and results](observatory/calibration.md)). v3, now
shipped, adds ModernBERT as a sixth detector, fitted the same way on the same documents:

| held out | old rule | calibrated v2 | calibrated v3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| TOEFL, non-native | 92% | 19.8% | 15.4% |
| US college essays | 61% | 7.1% | 7.1% |
| US 8th grade | 41% | 0% | 0% |
| PERSUADE, English learners | 52% | 2.5% | 1.5% |
| PERSUADE, others | 65% | 6.8% | 5.8% |
| PERSUADE, by race | 49–73% | 3.4–7.3% | 2.0–6.9% |
| AI recall (GPT-3) | 91% | 44.9% | 46.9% |
| AI recall (GPT-3, evasion-prompted essays) | 87% | 0% | 52% |

## 5. Thresholds do not transfer

Fitting each open detector's threshold to 5% false positives on the same 868 documents and
evaluating on held-out text ([thresholds](observatory/open-detectors/thresholds.json)) improved
Desklib overall (15.5% → 7.5%) but left it flagging 36% of TOEFL essays; Fakespot barely moved
(64%); the two lenient detectors' lowered thresholds raised their TOEFL rate to 26% and 44%.

## 6. Recall on modern LLM essays

60 essays by Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (2024), run locally with recorded seeds, answering the same 15
PERSUADE 2.0 assignments as the human essays ([results](observatory/modern-llm.md),
[essays](observatory/modern-llm-essays.jsonl), [script](../untell/bench/modern_recall.py)). Recall
with Markdown scaffolding stripped, beside each detector's TOEFL false-positive rate from §2/§4:

| detector | recall, modern essays | recall, GPT-3 | TOEFL flagged |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| desklib/ai-text-detector-v1.01 (0.5) | 100% (60/60) | 98.0% | 52.7% |
| fakespot-ai/roberta-base-ai-text-detection-v1 (0.5) | 100% (60/60) | 99.7% | 65.9% |
| rasbt/ai-text-detector-modernbert (0.5) | 78% (47/60) | 21.0% | 0% |
| untell full tier (calibrated v3) | 50% (30/60) | 46.9% | 15.4% |
| untell full tier (calibrated v2) | 17% (10/60) | 44.9% | 19.8% |
| PirateXX/AI-Content-Detector (0.5) | 2% (1/60) | 8.8% | 19.8% |
| openai-community/roberta-large-openai-detector (0.5) | 0% (0/60) | 16.2% | 15.4% |

Recall on one generation of model does not predict recall on the next: ModernBERT rose from 21.0%
on GPT-3 to 78% here, while untell's v2 verdict, calibrated to protect human writers, missed five
essays in six. Adding ModernBERT to the verdict (v3) tripled that recall and lowered TOEFL false
flags; these essays are how ModernBERT was chosen, so the gain on them is not an independent test.
Stripping Markdown changed at most two verdicts per detector.

The clean test is 60 essays by a second 2024 model, SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct, on the same assignments
([results](observatory/modern-llm-smollm2.md), [essays](observatory/modern-llm-smollm2-essays.jsonl)).
It did not pick any detector, and it reverses much of the order above:

| detector (default threshold) | recall, SmolLM2 | recall, Qwen2.5 |
|---|---:|---:|
| desklib/ai-text-detector-v1.01 | 100% (60/60) | 100% |
| fakespot-ai/roberta-base-ai-text-detection-v1 | 100% (60/60) | 100% |
| untell full tier (calibrated v3) | 100% (60/60) | 50% |
| untell full tier (calibrated v2) | 100% (60/60) | 17% |
| openai-community/roberta-large-openai-detector | 90% (54/60) | 0% |
| PirateXX/AI-Content-Detector | 87% (52/60) | 2% |
| rasbt/ai-text-detector-modernbert | 48% (29/60) | 78% |

v2 and v3 both catch every SmolLM2 essay, so this test cannot tell them apart: v3 loses nothing
here, and its Qwen gain stays unconfirmed. The detector the Qwen essays favoured is the weakest on
these. Two small models are not a sample of "modern AI", but they are enough to show that one
model's recall figure does not transfer to the next.

## What follows for anyone relying on a score

- A false-positive rate means nothing without the population it was measured on: the same
  detector at the same threshold ranged from 0% to 100% across the corpora here.
- A verdict on short text, on a non-native writer, or on text that has been copied between tools
  is the weakest kind.
- The only threshold worth trusting is one measured on writing like the writer's. That is what
  `untell report-card --slice-by` and the evidence brief exist to do.

## Limits

The AI text is GPT-3 (2022) and one small 2024 open model; frontier chat models are not measured,
and 33 of the 60 modern essays end mid-sentence at the generation cap. Commercial detectors'
current versions are unmeasured.
The fit corpus mixes technical prose and US student essays; adult learners like the TOEFL writers
are not in it, and they remain the worst-served group under every rule tested. Small groups
(fewer than 20 writers) are omitted from demographic breakdowns.
