Detector Evidence Kit

For a student, advisor, counsel or integrity officer looking at an AI-detector verdict. It tests the detector's verdict, explains what a flag is worth, and prints a short evidence brief.

What this is

An AI detector gives one number for one document. That number is being used as evidence. This page lets you check two things about it. Is the verdict stable? If the same words, with different quote marks or line breaks, get a different verdict, the verdict is reacting to formatting rather than authorship. What does a flag mean? Even a detector with a low false-positive rate flags honest writers, and the chance that a flagged document is human-written depends on numbers the score itself does not show.

This is a kit for testing a verdict. It is not a way to evade a detector. The "null edits" below change no words, so they cannot make AI-written text read as human in any meaningful sense. They only show whether the verdict is about the words.

Your text never leaves your device. Everything on this page runs in your browser. The page makes no network requests (its Content Security Policy forbids them), sends nothing to untell or anyone else, and saves nothing unless you tick "remember my text on this device". You copy each version into the detector yourself.

1Does the verdict survive edits that change no words?

Paste the text that was flagged. The page makes same-words versions (N1–N12) that change only formatting: quote style, spacing, line wrapping, invisible characters. Each one is checked here to contain exactly the same words in the same order. A second tier (L1–L4) makes light edits a human editor might make: contractions, the Oxford comma, US/UK spelling.

Run the original and each version through the detector in question. Record the score and whether it was flagged. The page then works out how often the verdict changed.

Versions to test

Score: the detector's number as it shows it (e.g. 87 for "87% AI"). Verdict: what the detector said. "No-op" means the edit found nothing to change, so that version is identical to the original and is left out of the counts.

2What a flag actually means

"The detector is 99% accurate" does not mean "there is a 99% chance this was AI". Reading it that way is the prosecutor's fallacy. Whether a flagged document is likely to be human-written depends on the detector's false-positive rate and on how common AI-written work is in this setting. And anyone who submits many documents gets many chances to be flagged by mistake.

The roll-up assumes each document is an independent draw at the stated false-positive rate. In practice errors cluster: a writer whose style a detector dislikes (non-native English, formulaic genres, heavy editing tools) is flagged far more often than the average rate, so some honest writers are flagged repeatedly. The base rate is a judgement call. Try several values.

3Printable evidence brief

Put the results above into a one-page brief you can print, save as PDF, or download as Markdown. The brief includes the word count but not your text. It also gives a SHA-256 fingerprint, so a reader can confirm which text was tested without seeing it.
